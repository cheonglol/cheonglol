## The experimenting tax

People assume AI makes you fast. Less discussed: before it's even fast, there's so much experimenting needed.

> "Before it's even fast there's so much experimenting needed."

Prompt, test, fail, adjust. Read the error, ask again, try another angle. Each cycle is cheap. The number of cycles adds up. Speed comes only after you've burned the time figuring out what works.

## No wonder seniors use it better

The people who get the most out of AI tend to be the ones who already know what "right" looks like.

> "It's like no wonder seniors can make better use of it."

They know what to ask, what to question, and what to throw away. The tool didn't make them good. It amplified what they already had.

## The people who hate it

I've met some AI-hating seniors. Or really, people who hate new things in general.

> "I have met some AI-hating seniors or just people who hate new things (java 8 people) which is understandable but wow what a waste."

I get it. New tools are annoying. They change the game you spent years learning. But wow — what a waste.

## Can I fit in?

That makes me wonder whether I can fit in such places.

> "I'm not sure if I can fit in such places."

If the room has decided the new thing is bad before trying it, I'm not sure I belong there. I'd rather be where tools get used, badly at first, and get better.

## Learning is an eventually thing

For me, learning is more of an "eventually" thing. What matters is getting stuff done.

> "Learning will be more of an eventually thing to me so getting stuff done."

And the difference I keep noticing: people who care about getting it done right will go search how things should be done — even with AI.

> "Depending on the individual, if they care about it getting done right, that would set them to search how things should be done even with AI."

The care makes the difference, not the tool.

## The overthinker part

I'm an easily-worried overthinker. I find myself asking a lot of "what should I consider?" questions.

> "I find myself asking a lot of what to consider, as an easily-worried overthinker."

Annoying in person. With AI, it works out — there's always something willing to list the considerations with me.
